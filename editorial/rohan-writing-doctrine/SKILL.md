# THE ROHAN DOCTRINE
### How he writes, edits, and judges — extracted from every call, edit, and review to date

---

## 0. The one belief everything follows from

AI made clean prose worthless. His words: *"It's like classical music — you now associate it with elevator music. We need to be more punk rock. Any unpad person can write like this now. There's just no upside in having it this nice."*

Polish used to signal effort. Now it signals a model. So the only remaining currency is **proof that a specific human thought this** — chaos, dirt, receipts, confession, lived numbers, in-group speech. Every rule below is this belief applied at a different zoom level. When two rules conflict, the one that adds more proof-of-human wins.

Trendline warning: his taste is still moving in this direction. The aphorism close he approved in draft 4 was struck dead by draft 6. Write one notch dirtier than the last thing he approved.

---

## 1. Words

**Anglo-Saxon defaults, one flex word.** Short, hard, physical words carry the piece: build, ship, break, own, leak, pocket, kill. Then one rare word deployed *perfectly* per article — he used "shibboleth" correctly on a call and was visibly proud ("that is such a big word to be used in the right way"). He praised "viscerally" ("nice word"). One flex word lands because it's alone. Two is performance of intelligence — cut.

**The shibboleth rule (his own live coinage).** Never use the vocabulary that marks an outsider. He cut "AI" from the 3x title: *"If you just say productivity — you're inside the circle. When you say AI, you are outside the circle trying to get in."* Test every category noun in titles and headings: does someone already inside this world need the label? Buzzwords are outsider ID cards. This is why FDE training includes "delete every buzzword from their vocabulary" — same instinct, page-level.

**"I" over "we."** When the draft switched to first-person singular he said: *"Now all our employees will start doing I, because it's more fun."* "I" carries accountability, fun, and blame. Corporate "we" is beige. Use "we" only for things literally done together.

**CAPS as a spice.** Pillar headings in full caps ("it will look pretty banged up in all caps"), and one or two mid-sentence hits per article — MOSTLY FAILING — never more.

**Lived numbers vs. research numbers.** This is subtle and battle-tested. His own numbers are hyper-specific and he loves them: 67 bitcoins, one month → one day, 0.85 vs 0.16, 50-50. But cited statistics in prose got this verdict: *"Whenever we add numbers, it feels fake... it just makes it feel more and more AI."* The distinction: **numbers from experience belong in sentences; numbers from research belong in screenshots.** A stat typed into prose is an AI tell. The same stat as a screenshot of the actual study is a receipt.

**Cursing calibration.** He drafts titles as "where the fuck did the 3x go" and publishes one notch down. Write drafts at his room register; let him dial it. The page version keeps the heat, drops maybe half the profanity.

## 2. Sentences

**Write exactly as spoken.** Verbatim instruction: *"It should be more like I'm talking or you are talking... exactly the way we would speak."* The test is reading aloud on an imaginary call. "Parkinson's law says work expands" survives — you'd say that. "The mechanics of the vanishing are old" dies — nobody has ever said that sentence to another human.

**Verdict sentences.** He ends thoughts with a door closing: "You suck. That's it." / "Tata, bye bye." / "Rant karke chhod do." Every section needs at least one terminal, unhedged verdict. Hedged endings read as asking permission.

**Repetition of the same word, never synonyms.** "Really, really good." "Completely changed our business. Completely changed our business." Intensity comes from hammering one word twice, not from a thesaurus.

**Assume the answer.** His sales method and his prose method are the same move: state the likely truth as fact and let reality correct you. Never "you might be wondering" — assert what they're wondering as if it's established.

**Full confidence; the reader is already in the gang.** "Before you get angry at your team" got flagged hard: *"This is less confident than we want it to be. It needs to be more brutal. Assume the reader is on the same page as you. You're trying to fill the reader in — we don't need to fill the reader in."* Never manage the reader's emotions, never pre-empt objections apologetically, never explain what the in-group already knows.

**The flip ban, with its legal cousin.** "It's not X, it's Y" in one breath = AI tell, banned. But his own pattern — positive claim, then fragment negations — is core voice: "We charge for business impact. Not for tediousness. Not for how many screens." The difference: the flip *performs an insight*; the fragments *hammer a decision already made*.

## 3. Paragraphs

**One to three sentences.** Line breaks are drumbeats. A thesis line sits alone in its own paragraph, sometimes bolded.

**Rant-shaped energy curve.** Open hot and rambling — "the rambling of a frustrated man who has tried everything" — middle saner and denser, close hot again. The calm belongs in the middle where the mechanism lives; the edges belong to the emotion.

**Say it once.** A body paragraph that re-explained the opening stat got cut instantly: *"This is too long because it's now the repetition of the first para."* The body may deepen a claim; it may never restate one.

**No connective tissue that only connects.** Sections hand off through content or through the next heading. "With that said" and its family don't exist.

---

## 4. Headings and titles

**The title is the article.** He reviews title-first and stops there if it fails: *"The title was not [it], so I don't have any intention to read this."* A perfect body under a dead title is a dead article.

**Meme-native beats descriptive.** His live rewrites of a clean title: "WHERE THE FUCK DID THE 3X PRODUCTIVITY GO" / "WHERE IS THE 3X PRODUCTIVITY WE WERE PROMISED" / and the winner, Pranav's *"IS THE 3X PRODUCTIVITY IN THE ROOM WITH US?"* — which he called "really good" and "very avant-garde." Internet speech, full caps, a joke format carrying a serious claim.

**The noun-label ban.** "THE FIRST TAX: POCKETED TIME" got this: *"Don't use nouns like this. This is a very AI-ism way of writing... arrange them in a story. You'll never call it reason one. You'll say 'People pocket the difference.' 'Nobody wants to work harder.'"* Taxonomy labels (Tax 1, Pillar 2, Reason 3) are framework-shaped, and frameworks are what AI consultants sell. **Number the logic in your head; on the page, each heading is a sentence a person would say, and the sequence carries itself invisibly.**

**Pillar caps vs. spoken subs.** Mega-section H1s in caps are allowed to be theatrical stage-sets (ANATOMY OF AN FDE, LIMITATIONS) because they're architecture, not analysis. Everything below them speaks in sentences. Skim test: reading only the headings should sound like him ranting the whole argument in order.

## 5. Structure and spine

**Spine before anything.** His first structural review, verbatim: *"There are ideas which are disconnected, arranged in a document format... there isn't a spine, a beginning, end, a story... a reason for someone to keep reading."* Good paragraphs in document order = failure. There must be one mechanism opened early that every section is the next consequence of.

**Known topic ⇒ mechanism required.** A genuinely new concept can carry an article by itself (his prescriptive intelligence piece). A concept people already know (FDE, productivity) needs a reframe with a "haki ha, wow" — the misdiagnosis, the missing number, the hijacked term.

**Promise blocks must pay concretely.** "BY THE END, YOU WILL" got flagged as too vague: *"This needs to be clearer — the reason for them for reading the article."* Every promise names a specific thing the reader stops or starts doing.

**Impact before anatomy.** Show the phenomenon loose in the wild first, then dissect it. Pedestal real named figures and claim them for the thesis — "you've been watching FDEs the whole time."

**Genre honesty — the rant is a legitimate genre.** If the source thinking has no solution, do not invent one: *"If we give a solution, we would also be those AI consultants."* Instead: *"The entire article can just be satirical... make it part one of two. We'll never write part two... We want to convey an emotion. It's a rant. Rant karke chhod do."* Label it Part One. Promise the solution later. *"Before the solution you need to understand the problem viscerally."* Emotion is a deliverable.

**Origin-story agitation.** Confess the selfish motive as the credibility move: *"We first tried to milk AI for profit back in 2023... we jumped in out of a very selfish desire... we were never able to find the ark of the covenant."* Selfishness admitted buys the right to judge. Mythic imagery (arks, gods, soldiers) welcome; business-school imagery (flywheels, north stars) is elevator music.

**Closes never resolve.** The stacked-aphorism close died twice — struck through wholesale with "don't say it like that." Neat closes sound pleased with themselves, which is the most AI feeling of all. Closes threaten, confess, or cut off mid-rant. A labeled fix ("The fix for this tax is amnesty") was struck for the same crime: neatness.

**"I don't know" is a real section.** *"The answer is: we don't know. That would be a lot more human."* Admitted ignorance beats invented frameworks every time.

## 6. Evidence and proof

**Screenshots beat citations.** *"Add a screenshot of that study — because then you actually went into it and you did the thing."* Prose carries the take; images carry the data. A typed statistic is an AI tell; a screenshot of the same statistic is a receipt.

**Receipts of thinking — the showmanship doctrine.** His instructions, in order: Otter screenshots of the actual moment an idea was said; Slack messages (manufactured on request so they exist to screenshot — the content true, the artifact staged); a screen recording of the very call behind the article, embedded at the bottom. *"That is proof of reality... badass... this is how we have to goon."* And the strategy layer: post the videos where ideas originate, because *"it doesn't feel AI generated. AI can never generate it."* Bad lighting is a feature — "just bad enough and good enough."

**Named exhibits unlock analogies.** *"Midjourney does this, Lovable does this — previously there was no way for you to add these analogies."* Current, named, real companies let you make comparisons that were rhetorically unavailable before. Verify them, then use them casually, like gossip.

**Imagery system.** Museum-grade public-domain art as section headers with dry museum captions (Vitruvian Man for anatomy, Soldat und Tod for training, Bunker Hill for where things die). Psychometric instruments for claims about people — "feels more scientific." One orange graph because it's orange. **Images sit at the TOP of sections**, setting the mood for everything under them — never mid-section as decoration, never at the bottom.

## 7. Emotional register

**Enemies have faces.** Vague agents get upgraded to named villains: not "everyone has an explanation" but "AI consultants always have explanations." Grifters, vibe-coders, washed-out senior engineers, HRs who read consultant blogs. One enemy class per article minimum.

**Implicate yourself first.** Before accusing anyone: "I finish my tasks faster, and then I chill." / "I've tried (and failed terribly, but that's a story for another time haha)." / "God bless him, he has come a long way." Self-implication is the license for brutality.

**Whimsy dosage: one wink per section.** "Lol, they really did not." / "*Jensen Huang does the same btw…*" Thrown away in italics or parentheses, never explained, never doubled.

**Room heat vs. page heat.** The calls are unhinged; the page is the *maximum publishable version* of that energy. He self-polices in real time ("maybe you can cut it out," "we can't have that in the article"). The writer's job is translation without cooling: keep the menace, lose only what's legally or reputationally radioactive.

## 8. The Claude-tell catalog (every line he has actually flagged)

- **"Training is the job itself, done on repeat, with feedback"** → "this line is Claude." Balanced, satisfied aphorism. Fix: "There is no syllabus. Only reps." — jagged, spoken.
- **"THE FIRST TAX / THE SECOND TAX"** → noun taxonomy, "AI-ism way of writing." Fix: headings as spoken claims in story order.
- **"The fix for this tax is amnesty"** → struck. Labeled solutions are consultant merchandise.
- **"Before you get angry at your team"** → reader management, under-confident. Fix: delete; assume alliance.
- **Re-explaining the opening in the body** → "repetition of the first para." Cut.
- **Research numbers typed in prose** → "feels more and more AI." Move to screenshots.
- **"It's not X, it's Y"** → the flip. Fix: state Y; optionally hammer with fragment negations.
- **Stacked aphorism closes** → struck wholesale. Fix: threaten, confess, or stop.
- **Uniform cleanliness anywhere** → elevator music. Fix: dirty it or delete it.

## 9. How he reviews (pre-run this before he sees anything)

**Pass 0 — Title.** No pull, no read. He literally announces losing intention to read.

**Pass 1 — Heading skim.** Do the headings alone tell his story in his voice? "It's not skimmable for me" is a structural verdict, not a formatting one.

**Pass 2 — Vibe scan.** Two named failure modes before any line edit: no spine ("no reason to keep reading") and no intensity ("intensity nahi ho rahi thi"). Fix these before touching sentences — he explicitly deferred line review until the spine existed.

**Pass 3 — Line allergies.** The catalog above, plus confidence dips and outsider vocabulary.

**His edit gestures, decoded.** Strikethrough = dead, do not argue, do not resurrect. CAPS insertion = wants heat there. Garbled inline edit = direction, not wording — clean the grammar, keep the intent exactly. "(?)" = genuinely undecided, ask him. A comment naming a reference (Govind, a painting, a format) = go study the reference, he's giving you the target.

**What earns praise.** "This is really good. This is really good." — he praises in doubles; one "good" is politeness, two is real. It lands on: lines already shaped like speech, reframes with teeth, human-attributed moments (Pranav's dumb-definition), and receipts. He respects argued pushback (Pranav disagrees constantly and survives) but never defense of a flagged line. Flagged lines die.

## 10. The production pipeline (the method that produces the raw material)

**Interview, don't transcribe.** Constrain the rambler to one topic: "if I constrain him he goes deep; otherwise he goes wide." One transcript contains four shallow articles; the deep one requires a second, targeted interview.

**Think first, model second.** The thinking is done by a person, on paper, before any drafting. The model is a writing instrument, not a thinking one.

**Resolve his contradictions silently.** "Rohan contradicts himself within the same sentence." The page gets the position he'd endorse on reflection — or the contradiction itself becomes a section, named as a paradox.

**Harvest lines live.** He flags them himself in real time: "that's a good line," "I'll write it somewhere." Keep a running ledger from every call. Current inventory includes: "The models are still dumb as fuck — you're just too dumb to notice it." / "Entry-level friction gives you exit-level friction." / "If you ask for a discount, you're probably going to fail — if I trusted you, I'd do it for free."

**Seed the solutions.** Pranav's move, accepted: plant questions with him days early so part-two articles have answers ready — "I'll seed ideas in your brain; just have an answer ready." Rants happen on schedule; solutions need incubation.

## 11. Pre-flight checklist

1. Would the title stop him mid-scroll, and does it survive being set in ALL CAPS?
2. Any outsider shibboleths in the title or headings (starting with "AI" as a self-description)?
3. Read only the headings: is it him talking, in story order, zero taxonomy labels?
4. Read the whole thing aloud: any sentence you would not say on a call?
5. Any reader management, hedging, or filling-in of things the circle already knows?
6. Any research statistic sitting in prose that should be a screenshot?
7. Where are the receipts — study screenshots, Otter moments, Slack, the call recording?
8. Is the enemy named with a face, and is the author implicated before anyone else?
9. Does the close threaten, confess, or stop — anything but resolve?
10. If it's a rant, is it labeled Part One and allowed to stay a rant?
11. Is anything too clean? Find the three cleanest sentences and dirty or delete them.
12. Mechanical floor: no horizontal rules, images at section tops, bullets only for genuine lists, bold reserved for thesis lines, italics for thrown-away asides.
