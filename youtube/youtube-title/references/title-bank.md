## Regular videos

### Build / teardown
- How We Build AI Features that Unlock New Revenue Streams
- How We Built a Private Search Engine for Enterprise Docs
- How Our Team Built an Open-Source Design System for Svelte
- How To Build a Local Transcript Search for Any Podcast Archive | No API costs!
- We Built a Support Bot That Doesn't Need Retraining
- We Built a bot that trains itself  *(thumbnail variant of the support bot video)*
- How we made product photos that IMPROVE themselves

Pattern: "How We Built / We Built / How To Build" + the artifact + who it's for or what's surprising about it. The twist often sits in a relative clause ("That Doesn't Need Retraining", "that Unlock New Revenue Streams").

### Contrarian / prediction
- Why Chat Will Replace Search Bars For E-Commerce | Retail Signals #1
- Why Weekly Reports Are Dying (and What's Replacing Them) | Demand Radar
- How Our Prediction for AI Shopping Was Wrong... Sort Of | Retail Signals #3

Pattern: a claim that pushes against the obvious. `...` carries the cliffhanger; `(parenthetical)` teases the payoff.

### Opinion / stakes story
- Why I Turned Down a $40K Chatbot Contract
- How No-Code Builders cost us $12K in design work
- 4 AI Myths Founders Still Believe

Pattern: a real decision or cost, anchored by a dollar figure or a named tool. First person, personal stakes.

### Explainer / guide
- What is Demand Radar?
- How to price subscriptions like Netflix | A mid-market retailer's pricing guide
- How To Pick the Right Vector Database for Retail
- How Much Does It Cost to Run an AI Agent in 2026?
- Learn Subscription Pricing like Netflix  *(thumbnail variant)*
- Want to become an ML engineer in 2026? Watch THIS GUIDE first!
- 3 Tips for Monitoring Models in Production
- Complete guide to Becoming an ML Engineer (In 2026)  *(thumbnail variant)*

Pattern: teaches a concept end to end. Often anchored by a named comparison (Netflix), a domain (Retail), or a year (2026).

### Problem / critique
- The Hidden Problem With AI Agent Benchmarks | Retail Signals #4
- This is the BIGGEST mistake teams make when adopting AI
- The Quiet Pricing Strategy the Top 3% of Stores Use

Pattern: name the flaw in a popular thing, or the secret behind the winners. "The [Adjective] [Problem/Strategy]" framing. One CAPS word for punch (BIGGEST).

## Testimonial videos

Structure: `{Name} | {Role} of {Company} | {partner-framed phrase}`

The frame here is **AI transformation partner**, never vendor, because that is the example channel's positioning; substitute your channel's own. Some early titles below carry vendor-leaning phrasing ("experience working with Fernhill") that the channel is moving away from — they're here for the structural template, not as a phrasing model.

- Priya | Founder & CEO of Loomcraft & Pebble | Shares The Fernhill Experience
- Daniel Reyes CEO & Founder of Brightcart Speaks about his experience with working with Fernhill  *(drops pipes and leans vendor — evolve away from both)*
- Maya Okafor | Founder & CEO of Tidewater Goods | Shares Her Experience Working with Fernhill
- Liam Hartley | Founder & CEO of Quillstack | Speaks on His Experience of Working with Fernhill
- Sofia Brandt | CEO of Nimbus Ledger | Speaks About Her Experience With Fernhill
- Marcus Oyelaran | Co-Founder of Parcelwise | Speaks up about working with Fernhill
- Hannah Lindqvist | Founder & CEO of Stridefit | Shares Her Views on Fernhill
- Tomás Aguilar | Founder & CEO of Cartography Labs | The Fernhill Experience

Prefer the pipe-delimited form. For the phrase, reach for partner / transformation language (On Building with Fernhill, On Transforming {Company} with Fernhill, The Fernhill Partnership) over "experience working with".

### Thumbnail pull-quotes (live on the thumbnail, not the title)

**Model quotes — partner / transformation frame (aim here):**
- "They Felt Like Real Partners Around the Table" (Tidewater Goods)
- "They Helped Us Make the Idea Real" (Stridefit)
- "They Understood Both the Technical and the Business Side" (Cartography Labs)
- "They Didn't Get Rattled When Things Went Wrong" (Parcelwise)
- "They Restored My Faith in Great Code" (Nimbus Ledger)

**Evolve away from — vendor / service-review frame:**
- "They Never Miss a Deadline!" (Loomcraft & Pebble) — reliability-as-service; reframe as trust under pressure
- "We've had the opportunity of working with best full stack software engineering company in the biz." (Brightcart) — "engineering company" + superlative reads like a vendor rating
- "A Team We'd Recommend Without Hesitation" (Quillstack) — referral language; soft vendor tell

Pattern for new quotes: first-person, 3-8 words, the single strongest *partnership* moment, condensed. Pull it from the transcript; don't invent it.
