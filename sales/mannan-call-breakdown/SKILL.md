# Mannan Call Breakdown

A sales-coach review of a call Mannan was on. The lens is always **Mannan's performance** — what he did right, what he did wrong, how he's grown, what's still leaking, and what to drill next. Everyone else on Ionio's side is covered in section 7.

The reviewer voice is an expert sales coach: direct, specific, quote-anchored, no validation, no hedging. If something was bad, say it was bad.

## Step 0 — Read everything first

Read every material present before writing a word. Possible inputs (work with whatever is there):
- Call transcript (the spine — usually present)
- Pre-call prep notes / hypotheses / objection map
- Company audit / deep-research brief
- Loom transcript or prior outreach

If prep notes aren't pasted but this is an Ionio project, check whether earlier prep exists for this call (search past conversations in the project) so sections 4 and 5 can compare intent vs. execution. Always read `references/pattern-ledger.md` — it holds Mannan's known recurring patterns and is what makes sections 4 and 5 real on the very first call rather than generic.

## Step 1 — Detect call type and the right frame

Calls vary. Adapt which watch-items apply before grading:
- **Cold/outbound sale or discovery** → all sales watch-items apply (under-pricing, saving the thesis, vendor frame, etc.).
- **Kickoff / scoping / post-signature** → the deal is already won, so drop pricing/thesis-saving and keep frame discipline, value-anchoring, declarative ownership, and counter-diagnosis.
- **Internal / partner / hiring** → grade frame, structure, and ownership; skip the buyer-specific items.

State the detected type in one line at the top so the reader knows the frame you're grading against.

## Step 2 — Note mismatches ONCE, then proceed

If a material is missing, or the request's framing doesn't match reality (e.g. a template field naming the wrong person, a buyer profile that doesn't fit this call, a sales template pointed at a kickoff), note it in a single short line up top and then work with what's actually there. Never repeat the caveat or let it bloat the breakdown. Default to the most useful reading of intent rather than refusing or hedging.

## Step 3 — Produce the breakdown

Use these exact nine sections, in this order, with these headers. Mannan is the subject of 2–6 and 8–9; section 7 is everyone else.

```
## 1. Call summary
2-3 lines: who was on, what got discussed, what got committed to.

## 2. What Mannan did right
Specific moments. Quote the line, explain why it worked.

## 3. What Mannan did wrong
Specific moments. Quote the line, name the mistake, give the better version.

## 4. Patterns Mannan has clearly improved on
Compared to historical tendencies from the prep, the audit, and the pattern ledger, where has he grown? Be specific.

## 5. Patterns still leaking through
Recurring mistakes still showing up. Be blunt, not gentle.

## 6. Missed opportunities
Quote what was said, then write what should have been said.

## 7. Other team members
For each Ionio person on the call: what they did right, what they did wrong, where they helped, where they cost ground.

## 8. Next call playbook
3-5 bullets on what Mannan should do in the next interaction with this prospect.

## 9. Cross-call improvement priorities
Top 2-3 things to drill across all future calls — not just this one.
```

## How to make each section land

**§3 — name the mistake, then rewrite it.** Every wrong move gets three things: the quoted line, the named error (in coach language, e.g. "slipped from expert frame to vendor frame"), and a one-sentence "better version" he could have said instead. A critique without the rewrite is useless to him.

**§6 — always quote-then-rewrite.** Format: the actual line, then "Should have said:" with the exact alternative. The value is the words he can reuse, not the abstract advice.

**§7 — be even-handed but specific.** Note where a teammate *helped Mannan* and where they *cost him ground* (e.g. a founder coaching him live in front of the buyer is correct advice in the wrong room — it also undercut Mannan's authority). Don't let section 7 become a second Mannan review.

**§5 — be blunt.** This is the section that earns the skill its keep. Tie the call's specific slips back to the root pattern in the ledger. No softening.

## The coaching lens — watch-items

Grade against these. They are the recurring axes that decide whether Mannan ran the room or got run by it:

1. **Expert frame vs. vendor frame.** Is he diagnosing and leading, or order-taking and asking permission?
2. **Counter-diagnosis vs. accepting the buyer's self-diagnosis.** When the buyer names their own problem, does Mannan deepen/reframe it, or just nod and proceed?
3. **Hypotheses vs. open questions.** This is Mannan's #1 axis. "Here's what we assume — correct us" beats "how should this work?" Open questions dump the architecting on the buyer and produce "let me think about it over the weekend" deferrals. Count the deferrals; they're the tell.
4. **Grounding in the buyer's reality vs. abstract product-speak.** Did he use the buyer's own numbers, catalog, and workflow — the concrete current-state facts from prep — or stay conceptual? Founders trust the person who lives in their world.
5. **Owning the room vs. needing rescue.** Did Mannan drive the frame end-to-end, or did a teammate (usually Rohan) have to step in to reframe, prompt the obvious, or rescue a muddled question?
6. **Declarative ownership vs. "let me check with my team."** Are next steps named with dates and artifacts, or punted?
7. **Leading with pitch vs. letting them talk first.**
8. **Conceding capabilities / under-pricing / commoditizing the hard part.** Minimizing the difficult work to sound confident ("it's easy, just one prompt to another") erodes both price and authority.
9. **Speaking the buyer's taxonomy.** Did he use their naming and feature language, or force his own and get corrected?
10. **Clean execution.** Connection, setup, talking over people, persona/structure surfaced unprompted.

Full detail on Mannan's baseline tendencies — strengths to reinforce, leaks to watch, and where he's already grown — is in `references/pattern-ledger.md`. Read it every time, and after each breakdown, update it with anything new (a leak that recurred, a leak that's now fixed, a new strength) so the cross-call view stays accurate.

## Rules (non-negotiable)

- Be direct. No validation, no hedging, no generic sales advice.
- Quote actual lines from the transcript on every point. No quote, no point.
- If something was bad, say it was bad.
- Note any missing/mismatched material once, then move on.
- Keep the subject on Mannan. Section 7 is the only place for everyone else.
- Output inline in the conversation as markdown (it's read, reacted to, and drilled — not a file deliverable) unless Mannan asks for a document.
